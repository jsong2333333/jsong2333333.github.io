Jialin Song

Ph.D. Student, School of Computing Science, Simon Fraser University.

Burnaby, BC, Canada

jialin_song [at] sfu.ca

I am a third-year Ph.D. student in Computing Science at Simon Fraser University, where I work on the safety and reliability of machine learning systems, advised by Prof. Wuyang Chen.

My current research has two threads. The first is large language model safety: I build scalable benchmarks for stress-testing models against multi-turn conversational attacks, and study why safety training that holds up in a single turn erodes over a longer dialogue. The second is scientific machine learning: I develop data-efficient operator learning methods, physics-informed surrogates for high-performance computing workloads, and language models that formalize and reason about partial differential equations.

Before SFU I was a machine learning engineer at the International Computer Science Institute in Berkeley, where I worked on robust and scientific machine learning with Prof. Michael Mahoney. I spent the summer of 2025 and 2026 as a research intern with the Deep Learning Group at Microsoft Research, Redmond, WA, working with Xiaodong Liu and Weiwei Yang. I hold an M.Eng. in EECS from UC Berkeley and a B.S. in Computer Science and Robotics Engineering from Worcester Polytechnic Institute.

Feel free to reach out if you would like to talk about any of this.

selected publications

  1. ICML
    MultiBreak: A Scalable and Diverse Multi-turn Jailbreak Benchmark for Evaluating LLM Safety
    Jialin Song, X. Liu, W. Yang, and 4 more authors
    In International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML), 2026
    Abs arXiv

    A scalable multi-turn jailbreak benchmark built with active-learning-based data expansion across diverse harm categories, used to stress-test the safety behaviour of large language models and to expose persistent vulnerabilities to conversational attacks.

  2. ICML
    PDE-Controller: LLMs for Autoformalization and Reasoning of PDEs
    M. Soroco*, Jialin Song*, M. Xia, and 3 more authors
    In International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML), 2025
    Abs arXiv

    A large language model that converts natural-language descriptions of partial differential equations into formal temporal logic and executable control code, trained with a back-translation and auto-verification pipeline for data generation.

  3. NeurIPS
    Data-Efficient Operator Learning via Unsupervised Pretraining and In-Context Learning
    W. Chen*, Jialin Song*, P. Ren, and 3 more authors
    In Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS), 2024
    Abs arXiv

    An unsupervised pretraining and in-context learning pipeline that reduces the simulation data needed to train neural operators, studied across masking ratios, subsampling regimes and several PDE systems.
